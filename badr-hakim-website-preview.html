Working Principles

Four principles, no exceptions

These are not slogans — they are the rules that decided real cases, real cash drawers, and real escalations across six years.

Compliance Before Convenience

KYC, AML, and FATCA/CRS checks are never the step you skip to save time. In banking, the shortcut is always the most expensive route — for the client, the bank, and your own record. Every interaction I handle passes verification first, resolution second.

Accuracy Is a Form of Respect

A balanced cash drawer, a correctly logged case, a reconciliation with zero unexplained difference — these are how a bank shows clients their money is taken seriously. I count twice, document once, and never leave a discrepancy "for tomorrow."

Explain It Like a Human

Clients rarely need more banking jargon; they need to understand what happened to their account, card, or transfer — in Arabic or English, in plain words. Clear explanation is what turns a frustrated caller into a loyal customer.

The Team Carries the Standard

As a supervisor I learned that quality is a team habit, not an individual talent. Shared checklists, honest QA feedback, and coaching after hard calls are what keep hundreds of daily interactions at one standard.

Two Systems, One Standard

Conventional and Islamic banking

Working at the Bank of Jordan (conventional) and Dubai Islamic Bank and the Syrian International Islamic Bank (Islamic) gave me both operating models from the inside: interest-based products on one side, Sharia-compliant structures on the other — with the same expectations of accuracy, documentation, and client care in both.

That dual fluency matters for employers who serve mixed client bases, and for clients who move between systems and need someone who can explain the difference honestly.

Languages in Practice

Arabic — fluent. Native-level service, documentation, and client communication, including the dialects of the Levant and the Gulf clients served in the UAE.

English — advanced. Professional correspondence, banking terminology, reporting, and cross-border client service in writing and speech.

Being bilingual is not a line on a CV — at a contact center handling thousands of inquiries, it is the difference between a case resolved in one call and one that bounces between agents.
